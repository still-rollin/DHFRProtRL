DHFRProtRL review

What the LatProtRL port to the DHFR dataset shows, checked against the raw result files. 9 October 2026. Every fitness value on this page is a prediction from a surrogate model, normalized to 0–1.

The pipeline works and its numbers are consistent. Re-scoring the best sequence of each of the 13 multi-round runs reproduces the saved results to within 0.005.

The RL does not beat simple search. Scanning the surrogate model over every single mutation finds a sequence scoring 0.979 in 4,161 calls. The best RL run reached 0.866 in 38,400.

The runs converge on the same few substitutions. The four that recur most (V116I, K106N, Y196W, V160I) are each already in the top 6% of measured single mutants. The agent combines known-good singles.

These are conclusions about the surrogate model, which was trained on 85% of the same variants that define the optimum, including the best one. They say nothing yet about real enzyme activity.

Where those scores sit in the data

The dataset is a complete single-substitution scan: 219 positions × 19 substitutions, 4,161 variants, each one change from a reference sequence that is not itself in the file. 150 of them (3.6%) score 0.738 or more. That is the value the working notes call wild-type, but it is actually the label of one mutant, D1A.

What is and is not established

Established

  • Saved metrics are internally consistent: 13 of 13 re-scored sequences agree within 0.005.
  • The model’s correlation with the labels over all 4,161 variants (0.935) reproduces. On its held-out test set it is 0.742.
  • PPO and SAC reach the same small set of mutations (V116I in 11 of 13 runs, K106N or K106D in 10).
  • Under this model, simple baselines score higher than every RL run.
  • The model scores the 13 best multi-mutant sequences slightly below a simple additive estimate from single-mutant labels (median −0.26 raw units).

Not established

  • Any real activity. There is no second model, structure or stability prediction, or lab data.
  • That the model is accurate on new sequences. Its held-out Pearson is 0.742, not 0.935, and the training script never restores its best epoch. A retrained copy scored 0.14 on positions it had not seen.
  • That any multi-mutant is actually good. The data holds single mutants only.
  • That the dense reward fixed a failure. The model was loaded wrongly for part of the project, and no run compares old and new reward on the corrected model.
  • Any gain smaller than the model’s own error on unseen variants (about 0.10 on the 0–1 scale), or any comparison between runs less than about 0.01 apart. Most settings ran once, for 4 to 10 rounds.

What would turn this into a result

In order of how much each one settles.

  1. Hide the best variants from the model and see if the agent finds them. Retrain the model without the top-scoring single mutants. If the agent still reaches them, that is discovery. Today the model very likely already knows where the optimum is.
  2. Retrain the model with working early stopping, report its held-out accuracy, and score the final sequences with a second, independent model (an ensemble would also show where it is unsure).
  3. Compare old and new reward on the corrected model, with several seeds and the full 15 rounds.
  4. Add the upstream baselines (PEX, AdaLead, CMA-ES) on this dataset.
  5. Get multi-mutant measurements for the top candidates, even a few, to check the additive behaviour.
